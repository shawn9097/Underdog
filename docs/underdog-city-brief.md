# UNDERDOG CITY — PROJECT BRIEF

*Single source of truth: brand + story + music. Consolidated from the earlier
master brief and the "v0.1" anime bible, with the song catalog corrected against

---

## 0. WHAT THIS IS

**Underdog City** is a transmedia dark-fantasy IP fronted by a masked, anonymous
music artist. The music, the story (anime/novel), and the brand are **one
object**, not three projects.

**Core thesis:** *The world throws people away. Down here, the broken discover
that broken things hold the most power — and build a kingdom out of everything
that was discarded.*

**Audience promise:** make the misfits and outcasts lovable, relatable, and
powerful. Give the discarded a home and a crown.

## 1. BRAND CORE

- **Artist / project name:** Underdog City
- **Fan community:** The Underdogs
- **Manifesto:** *"We all rule down here. There's only one rule in Underdog City:
  turn that shit up loud."*
- **Email-capture hook:** *"Now Accepting Tenants in Underdog City."*
- **Persona principle:** the artist is **masked and anonymous**. The face is
  never shown (yet). The masked MC of the story and the masked artist are the
  same identity. Mystery is a feature.
- **Tone:** dark, defiant, cathartic, literary, anti-redemption, found-family
  heart under a hard shell. Confrontational but emotionally honest.
- **Tagline candidates:** "We all rule down here." / "Now accepting tenants." /
  "Throne at the bottom."

### Motifs

- **Kintsugi** — cracks repaired/lit with gold. The signature visual + the magic
  system.
- **The broken crown** — shattered and reforged, jagged, asymmetric. Not clean
  royalty.
- **The dark throne** — built at the bottom.
- **The mask** — matte-black, fractured with gold veins.

## 2. THE STORY WORLD (anime / novel bible)

### Tone blend

Gachiakuta (the discarded rise) × Cyberpunk: Edgerunners (neon underclass,
doomed glory, found family) × Dorohedoro (grimy surreal underworld, masks, dark
humor) × The Legend of Vox Machina (misfit ensemble, crude-funny heart). Prose
voice = **Rebecca Yarros × Dean Koontz** (propulsive, voice-y, emotionally
direct, atmospheric dread).

### The world

- **THE HALO** — gleaming elite ring above. Run by **the Sainted (Saints)**, who
  decide who has worth. Cold, self-righteous, redemption-as-product.
- **UNDERDOG CITY** — the sprawl below where the Dropped land. Grimy, surreal,
  neon-and-garbage. Misfit-run districts. *"We all rule down here."*
- **THE DROP** — when the Halo deems you worthless, you're cast down.

### Magic system — KINTSUGI

- Hit absolute bottom + survive the break → gold runs through the fracture → you
  **gild**: a power tied to the exact wound that broke you. *Your damage is your
  weapon.*
- Cost: each use spreads the gold; burn out and you **shatter** (death) or go
  **hollow** and *ascend* — becoming a husk the Halo reclaims as one of the
  Sainted. **Salvation is the real horror.**
- Visual: cracked skin lit with molten gold; crown motif on those who've accepted
  their fracture.

### The MC (Jinwoo × Benimaru)

- Most thrown-away person in the city. Dropped, **died at the bottom, Came Back
  Wrong.**
- **Gild:** can call on the discarded (the broken, the forgotten, the cast-off —
  a shadow-army of the rejected).
- **Drive:** protection, not conquest. Builds a throne at the bottom to give the
  unwanted a home and a crown — the landlord-king of misfits (*"Now Accepting
  Tenants"*).
- **Memory: fractured.** Came back with holes; doesn't know who X is, who killed
  him, or the face the vow was made to. Hunts the same truth as the audience;
  full reveal lands on MC and audience together at the Season 1 finale.
- **Masked.** Face hidden/changed. The artist persona = this character.

### Format plan for the book

Web serial / light novel (Royal Road / Scribble Hub / Wattpad / Tapas). Proven
pipeline: web novel → webtoon → anime (Solo Leveling's path). Serialized chapters
double as pre-release lore content.

## Appendix A — Story beats (preserved from the earlier drafts)

*Kept for reference; these are the deeper narrative beats trimmed out of the core
brief above. Where the two source docs conflicted, the newer/refined version is
used.*

### Episode 1 — cold open (locked)

- The audience sees feet/ankles and a shadow slumping into the MC's arms, blood
  dripping. It reads as: the MC stood over a body he caused.
- **The truth:** the body **IS** the MC — he's the one who died, trying to protect
  **X**, and **failed.** The real killer (protected by the Halo) is **framed as a
  hero and celebrated**; the dead outcast is branded the villain. The Halo launders
  a murder into heroism and a hero into a monster.
- The MC **gilds by dying** and comes back into a world that already calls him the
  villain. He stops correcting it — becomes the monster on purpose, as armor and
  weapon.
- **Buried clue (fair play):** something of X's is physically pressed into his hand
  in the cold open. On his return, his hand is **empty** — the lost object is the
  Season 1 mystery thread.

### MC — memory (locked: fractured)

- Came back with holes. Knows he was wronged, knows the rage; does not have full
  access to who X is, who killed him, or the face the vow was made to.
- Hunts the same truth the audience is; the full memory lands on MC and audience
  **simultaneously** at the Season 1 finale.
- Fragments surface through proximity and emotion, not exposition — every meaningful
  relationship is also a potential memory trigger.

### The love interest

- From the Halo; not a true believer, cracking from the inside. Handed the villain
  story about the MC and told to believe it.
- Drawn to him; keeps pulling at the thread that doesn't fit. She hunts the truth
  from the **opposite direction** as the audience.
- **Unmasking scene (Season 1 cliffhanger):** he removes the mask not as proof of
  innocence but as an act of trust with no safety net — *"I swore I'd never be the
  monster you could see."* He doesn't know if the vow was made to *her*. Open
  question: does she hold the memory he's missing, or take what she's learned back
  to the Halo?

>
> "Run," I tell her. "I'm fine. Go."
>
> I'm not fine. I'm flat on my back in a service corridor sixty levels under the Halo, and the warmth spreading beneath me is the last warm thing I'm ever going to feel. We both know it. But she doesn't move — she never does what she's told — so I say it again, uglier this time, because ugly is the only thing that's ever made her listen.
>
> "Go. Before the bell stops ringing."
>

The monster crawled up out of the dark. The hero put it down. Sleep well.
>
> The man with the clean boots is already gone. He didn't even stay to watch me fall. That's the part that should make me angry, and it does — but anger costs blood, and I'm running low.
>

>
> She says my name. I feel it more than hear it. It lands somewhere in my chest and cracks straight down the middle.
>

>
> I'm about to break that promise. I can feel it coming the way you feel a storm in your teeth.
>
> She takes my hand. Presses something into it — small, hard, warm from her grip — and folds my fingers shut around it like a secret she's trusting me to keep alive when she can't. I try to look at it. My eyes won't go where I send them.

>

> The cold comes up through the warm. The corridor lights slide from gold to white to gone. The last thing left in the world is her face, and even that's pulling away from me now, the edges blurring, the name behind it dissolving like a word said too many times—

> I'm wrong.
>

>
> Something moves behind my ribs. Something that wasn't there a minute ago.
>
> Something gold. And patient. And very, very angry.
>
> I don't remember my name. I don't remember her face — only that there was a her, that she mattered more than my own life did, that I spent the last of myself trying to keep her breathing. I don't remember the promise. My hand is open and empty and I can't remember what was in it, only that losing it hurts worse than the dying did.
>

>

> Fine.
